# Where the facts are

Read this when a data trail goes cold. The point of the list isn't completeness — it's to stop you settling for a press article when a filing exists.

## Filings and registries

| Jurisdiction | Source | Holds |
|---|---|---|
| US listed | SEC EDGAR | 10-K, 10-Q, 8-K, DEF 14A (proxy: exec pay, board, ownership), S-1 |
| EU/France | AMF (BALO), company IR site | Universal Registration Document (URD) — the single richest doc for a French listed group: strategy, risk factors, exec comp, org, shareholders |
| France private | Infogreffe / Pappers / BODACC | Statutory accounts, directors, beneficial owners, insolvency notices |
| UK | Companies House | Accounts, PSC (persons with significant control), officers, charges |
| Germany | Bundesanzeiger / Unternehmensregister | Jahresabschluss |
| Netherlands / Belgium / Nordics | KVK / NBB / Bolagsverket | Statutory accounts |
| EU-wide | national business registers via BRIS | Basic company data |

Listed companies also publish: annual report, results presentation deck (often the clearest strategy statement anywhere), capital-markets-day materials, and earnings-call transcripts. The CMD deck is usually where management states the strategy plainly and puts numbers on it.

## Regulated sectors — the register is free intelligence

- **Banking/payments (EU)**: EBA credit institutions register, national regulator registers (ACPR REGAFI in France, BaFin, DNB, FCA in UK), EU PSD2 payment/e-money institution registers. Licence type tells you what a company is legally allowed to do — often a harder constraint than strategy.
- **Insurance**: EIOPA, national supervisors, Solvency II SFCR reports (public, detailed).
- **Health/pharma**: EMA/FDA approvals, clinical trial registers.
- **Telecom/energy**: national regulator licence registers.

## Startups and companies with no financials

| Signal | Source |
|---|---|
| Funding, valuation, investors | Company press releases (most reliable), TechCrunch/Sifted/trade press, Crunchbase, investor portfolio pages |
| Cap table and control | National register (UK PSC, France beneficial owners), press at round announcements |
| Headcount and trend | LinkedIn company page (and the "employees" count over time if you can find archived snapshots) |
| Hiring strategy | Careers page — count roles by function and geography. Where they hire is where they're going. |
| Business model | Pricing page, T&Cs, developer docs, API reference |
| Traction | Customer logos, case studies, integration/partner directories, app-store review counts and ranks |
| Product velocity | Changelog, release notes, app-store update cadence, GitHub activity |
| IP | Espacenet, EUIPO/USPTO trademark search |
| Demand | App-store rankings, public web-traffic estimators (treat as `[C]`, always) |

## Consortium / JV vehicles (EPI, Swift, Zelle, Vipps-type)

There is often no useful P&L, and looking for one is a waste of time. The real intelligence is:

- **Member list and any changes to it.** Members leaving is the loudest possible signal.
- **Funding commitments** announced by the members (they disclose in their own reports even when the vehicle doesn't).
- **Governance**: board seats, who chairs, whether decisions need unanimity.
- **Mandate**: what the members built it *for*. Usually defensive — a response to a competitive or regulatory threat. Name the threat.
- **Regulatory tailwind**: for EU payments, instant-payments regulation, PSD3, digital-euro workstreams and the Digital Markets Act all shape the operating environment more than any commercial decision the vehicle makes.

Treat the shareholders' own annual reports as primary sources on the vehicle. A bank's annual report will describe its stake and often its contribution.

## Executives

- Company site "governance"/"leadership" page (authoritative on titles and structure)
- Annual report — org and exec committee composition, plus tenure and comp
- LinkedIn — tenure, prior roles, and often the reporting line implied by how they describe the job
- Conference speaker bios and press interviews — where digital leaders say what they're actually doing
- Press releases announcing appointments — these state the reporting line explicitly, which the website usually doesn't

## Finding the conversation hook (latest digital news)

Run these searches deliberately, once you already understand the business — relevance is a judgement you can't make cold:

- `"<company>" digital OR technology OR AI` restricted to the last 3–6 months
- `"<company>" acquisition OR partnership OR launch OR platform` — recent
- `"<digital exec name>"` — interviews, keynotes, LinkedIn posts. Executives telegraph strategy in public far more than they realise.
- The company's own newsroom, filtered to tech/digital
- Latest results-call transcript or presentation — search it for "digital", "AI", "technology": what management volunteers under scrutiny is the most reliable statement of priority you'll get
- Trade press for the sector (payments: Finextra, The Paypers; banking: Sifted, Les Échos) — they cover things the generalist press misses

Judge each candidate against one test: **would this make the other person in the room lean forward?** A disposal, an outage, a big hire, a target they've publicly committed to — yes. An award or a rebrand — no.

## Hygiene

- Prefer primary (filing, register, company statement) over secondary (article about a filing).
- Date every fact. A 2021 headcount presented as current is a lie of omission.
- When two sources disagree, say so in the source log rather than silently picking one.
