# Scoring rubric

Anchors exist so a 3 means the same thing in March and in November, and across two different
companies. Score against the anchor, not against your impression of the founder.

**Default weights** (adjust to the fund's thesis and state the change in `total_note`):

| Module | Pre-seed / Seed | Series A/B | Growth / PE |
|---|---|---|---|
| Problem & value proposition | 3 | 1 | 1 |
| Segmentation & ICP | 1 | 2 | 2 |
| PMF evidence | 2 | 3 | 3 |
| Market & sizing | 2 | 2 | 2 |
| Differentiation & moat | 2 | 2 | 3 |
| Monetization | 1 | 2 | 3 |
| GTM–product fit | 1 | 2 | 3 |
| Product org & velocity | 2 | 1 | 2 |

At pre-seed you are scoring **insight and evidence of learning**, because retention data doesn't exist
yet. Past seed, absence of clean cohort data is itself a finding — score PMF evidence low and say why.

---

## 1. Problem & value proposition
- **0–1** Solution looking for a problem. Customers can't articulate what it replaces.
- **2** Real but mild pain; a vitamin. Bought from discretionary budget, first to be cut.
- **3** Acute pain for a defined user, but the buyer doesn't feel it — long sales cycles.
- **4** Convergent language across 5+ customers; replaces something specific and costly.
- **5** Customers describe it as infrastructure; they built workarounds before it existed.

## 2. Segmentation & ICP
- **0–1** No ICP. "Anyone with the problem." Revenue spread across incoherent segments.
- **2** ICP stated but contradicted by the pipeline; blended metrics hide divergence.
- **3** ICP defined firmographically, roughly right, not yet behavioural.
- **4** Segments defined by behaviour and willingness to pay; sales targets the retaining one.
- **5** Above, plus deliberate de-selection — they turn away bad-fit revenue and can prove it.

## 3. PMF evidence
- **0–1** Curve falling throughout; growth entirely paid; churn exceeds expansion.
- **2** Curve still declining at the observed horizon; NRR < 90%; one segment carries everything.
- **3** Curve flattens for the core segment but not blended; NRR 90–100%.
- **4** Clear plateau for the ICP; NRR 100–120%; meaningful organic acquisition.
- **5** Plateau plus a rising curve (revenue retention > 100% on the cohort); usage frequency matches
  the natural frequency of the job; measurable pull.

## 4. Market & sizing
- **0–1** Top-down TAM only. No bottom-up logic survives contact.
- **2** Bottom-up exists but rests on penetration assumptions with no precedent.
- **3** Credible SOM; reaching it needs a channel they don't have yet, acknowledged.
- **4** Bottom-up sourced factor by factor; current traction is evidence the path works.
- **5** Above, plus an adjacency that is a genuine second act, not a slide.

## 5. Differentiation & moat
- **0–1** Feature parity with cheaper incumbents; only differentiator is price or effort.
- **2** Real product edge today, fully copyable within 12 months.
- **3** Workflow entrenchment building; switching costs exist but are not yet painful.
- **4** A structural mechanism is compounding — proprietary data improving the product, integrations
  that get costlier to unwind, or a distribution position competitors can't buy.
- **5** The mechanism is measurable: show the metric that gets better as the company gets bigger.

## 6. Monetization
- **0–1** Price set by gut, never tested; heavy discounting; metric unrelated to value.
- **2** Pricing metric decoupled from value — customers can 3x their value at flat cost.
- **3** Metric roughly aligned; packaging muddled; expansion happens but isn't engineered.
- **4** Value metric and pricing metric align; expansion is a designed motion; discounting disciplined.
- **5** Above, plus evidence from a real price test, not a survey.

## 7. GTM–product fit
- **0–1** Motion contradicts the ACV; CAC payback beyond 36 months or unmeasured.
- **2** Payback 24–36m, or a single channel doing all acquisition with no second one working.
- **3** Payback 18–24m; product sellable by reps but founder still closes the big ones.
- **4** Payback < 18m in the core segment; two channels working; time-to-first-value measured in days.
- **5** Payback < 12m; product sells itself far enough that sales expands rather than persuades.

## 8. Product org & velocity
- **0–1** No discovery; roadmap is the largest customer's backlog; shipping has stalled.
- **2** Ships steadily but builds what's asked for; no evidence of user contact by builders.
- **3** Discovery happens; roadmap traceable to strategy; tech debt acknowledged and unsized.
- **4** Regular user contact, decisions reversed on evidence, debt sized and budgeted.
- **5** Above, plus the team has killed something significant for the right reason.

---

## Reading the total

| Weighted | Meaning |
|---|---|
| < 2.5 | Pass — the issues are structural, not executional |
| 2.5–3.4 | Conditional — name the two or three specific, testable conditions |
| 3.5–4.2 | Invest, with a value-creation plan (hand to `/valuecreation`) |
| > 4.2 | Invest; the risk is price, not product |

State the bar in the memo. A score with no threshold attached is decoration.
